Thesis Project

The Effect of Risk-Based Capital (RBC) and Underwriting Ratio on Return on Assets (ROA) at PT Asuransi Bhakti Bhayangkara (2020–2024)

Undergraduate thesis | UIN Sunan Gunung Djati Bandung | May 2026 | Grade A

Read the Thesis

ROA at the insurer fell from 1.88% to 0.79% between 2020 and 2024 (charts at the top of this page). I tested whether two measures, capital strength and underwriting performance, explain its monthly profitability. The data are 60 monthly observations from the company’s financial reports.

  • RBC = solvency margin ÷ minimum required capital. The regulatory minimum is 120% (POJK 72/2016).
  • Underwriting ratio = underwriting result ÷ net premium.
  • ROA = net profit ÷ total assets.

My SPSS and Gretl Output

What the Results Say

  • Both RBC and the underwriting ratio have a positive, significant effect on ROA (p < 0.001).
  • Their standardized betas are close (0.513 and 0.502), so the two carry similar weight once their different scales are accounted for.
  • Together they explain 57.2% of ROA variation. The other 42.8% comes from factors outside the model, such as investment income.
  • This shows association within one company over five years. It does not prove cause.

Islamic Business Ethics

I interviewed the company’s actuary and its head of accounting. They described capital as protection for policyholders (amanah and justice), underwriting as fair, non-discriminatory pricing with honest claim handling, and profit as something to pursue within OJK rules. The company is conventional, so the study describes how its practices reflect these principles. It does not judge the Sharia compliance of its contracts.

Limits: one company, five years, and no investment-income variable.
